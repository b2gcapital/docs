This knowledge base was written for people who are getting to know Trepzy for the first time. You do not need to know how banks, digital currencies, or software systems work before you start. When a new term is necessary, it will be explained before the text moves on. This page tells you how to navigate the material efficiently and what to watch for as you read.

## Recommended reading order

Follow these six steps in sequence the first time through. After that, use the links between pages to jump back to whatever topic you need.

<Steps>
  <Step title="What Trepzy Is">
    Start with the company, the problem it wants to solve, and what it is building first. This gives you the frame for everything that follows.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fundamentals">
    Cover money, ways to send and receive it, digital currencies, security, and identity verification. These concepts appear throughout every journey section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Who Is Who">
    Understand the person who logs into the app, the account holder, and the people authorized to act on their behalf. Knowing these distinctions prevents misreading any journey.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Product">
    Learn how someone opens an account and which features they can use. This is where the customer-facing experience is described in detail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Journeys">
    Walk through what happens from start to finish when someone receives or sends value — including what happens when a step gets stuck.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Partner Companies">
    Meet the companies that verify documents, hold resources, or execute parts of a movement. Understand where each one fits in the overall structure.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Feature status labels

Three status labels appear throughout this knowledge base. Apply them carefully — they describe a specific function for a specific group, not the product as a whole.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Available Now" icon="circle-check">
    A person with the right authorization can use this function under the stated conditions. A feature that is available for a small group may not yet be open to all customers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="In Preparation" icon="circle-half-stroke">
    Trepzy is actively building or adjusting this function. Part of it may appear on a screen or work in testing, but the complete journey is not yet ready for customers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Under Study" icon="circle">
    A possibility exists, but decisions about format, responsible company, cost, and timing have not been made. Do not treat this as a commitment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
**Example in practice:** requesting an account to receive dollars, having the account details approved, and seeing a completed deposit are three separate events. Each one can carry a different status. The documentation will always tell you which event it is describing, for whom, and at what point in time.
</Note>

## What the customer sees vs. what the team needs to know

The customer sees a simple operation in a few taps: choose, review, authorize, track. Our team needs to understand the work happening behind that experience, including what depends on other companies.

Pages in this knowledge base will explicitly signal when they are describing **what the customer sees** and when they are explaining **the internal work that sustains that experience**. A map showing several companies and steps does not mean the customer has to walk through all of them or know their names.

## Six internal questions every journey section answers

As you read through the journey sections, each one is structured to answer these six questions. Keep them in mind — they are the fastest way to orient yourself within any given flow.

<Accordion title="Who is the holder?">
  Which person or company owns the account or resource involved in this step? Knowing the holder is the baseline for everything else.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who must authorize?">
  Which person (or which combination of people) must give approval before this step can proceed? Authorization rules vary by action and account type.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who executes each step?">
  Which company — Trepzy or a partner — actually performs the work at each point in the journey? This is the internal map the customer never needs to see.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where is the value?">
  At any given moment in the journey, in which account, system, or custody does the value sit? This question matters especially when an operation is in progress or stuck.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do we know a step finished?">
  What confirmation, signal, or state change tells us — and tells the customer — that a step has completed successfully?
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What can the person do if something stops?">
  If a step gets stuck or an error occurs, what options does the account holder have? What can the Trepzy team do, and what requires action from a partner?
</Accordion>
