When a customer opens a Conta Trepzy and starts moving money across borders, the experience looks simple from the outside. Behind it, several specialized companies each handle a distinct part of the journey — document verification, digital-asset custody, banking rail access, and more. This page defines what Trepzy means by "partner," explains why we need them, names the current ones, and draws a clear line between what partners do and what always stays with Trepzy.

<Note>
  The partner list reflects the current documented state of Trepzy's operations. New partners will be added as Trepzy expands its rails, currencies, and services. When a new partner is onboarded, this page should be updated before the relevant feature is described anywhere else in the knowledge base.
</Note>

## What "partner" means at Trepzy

In this knowledge base, **a partner is an external company that provides a specific part of a service used by Trepzy.** The word does not mean:

- A shareholder or investor in Trepzy.
- A member of a customer's own team.
- A company that sells products or services *through* the Trepzy platform.

Partners exist at the infrastructure layer. The customer interacts with a single Trepzy experience; our team needs to know which company is responsible for each underlying step.

## Why Trepzy uses partners

Certain steps in a financial operation require specializations — or regulated structures — that Trepzy does not hold on its own. Examples:

- **Document and biometric verification** requires a company with the tooling and legal authorization to certify that a document is authentic and that the person presenting it matches it.
- **Digital-asset custody and movement** requires infrastructure to control values recorded on digital networks, including the key management and signing that such movements demand.
- **Banking rail access and currency conversion** requires a financial institution or payment provider with the relationships and licenses to receive funds in a given currency and convert or route them to the right destination.

None of these partners do the same thing. Each one can request its own information, impose its own limits, and confirm only its own result. A positive outcome from one partner does not cascade into approval of the full operation — Trepzy assembles the pieces and decides what to communicate to the customer.

## Current partners and their roles

<Warning>
  A partner confirming their piece of an operation — for example, a successful identity check — does **not** mean the full operation is approved. Each partner approves only what is within their scope. Trepzy combines all results and determines the overall state the customer sees.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  When documenting a new feature or journey, always specify which partner executes each external step. Don't refer to "the verification partner" or "the financial partner" generically — name the company so the internal record is unambiguous.
</Tip>

## What "primary" and "secondary" mean

These labels describe **Trepzy's routing choice for a given operation type.** They are not:

- A ranking of partner quality, capability, or reliability.
- A statement about the partner's market position or product breadth.
- A permanent designation — routing choices can change as Trepzy's operations evolve.

**Primary** means Trepzy routes eligible operations through that partner by default. **Secondary** means Trepzy routes specific operation types — currently Pix — through that partner instead of (or in addition to) the primary. A customer never needs to know or choose between primary and secondary partners.

## What always stays with Trepzy

Regardless of which partner executes a step, the following responsibilities remain with Trepzy:

  <Card title="Customer relationship" icon="user">

  </Card>
  <Card title="State reporting" icon="chart-bar">
    Trepzy collects results from all partners and presents a single, coherent operation state. It must not surface a partner's partial result as a final answer, and must not report "completed" until all necessary steps have confirmed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Compliance decisions" icon="shield-check">

  <Card title="Explaining what happens next" icon="arrow-right">
    If an operation is pending, blocked, or failed, Trepzy must tell the customer why (to the extent permitted) and what action — if any — they need to take. The customer should never have to contact multiple companies to understand the state of a Trepzy journey.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Common mistakes to avoid

When writing about or communicating partner interactions internally, watch for these errors:

- **Conflating a partner's result with Trepzy's decision.** A document check passing is not the same as the account being approved.
- **Treating "primary" as "better."** BlindPay is the primary financial partner because of Trepzy's current routing architecture — not because it is superior to every alternative for every use case.
- **Assuming all partner capabilities are active.** A partner may support features that Trepzy has not yet enabled. Only describe what is confirmed for the specific customer segment and operation type you're documenting.
- **Presenting internal orchestration complexity to the customer.** A map of Trepzy's partner architecture is an internal tool. The customer should see a unified experience, not a list of companies handling their money.
