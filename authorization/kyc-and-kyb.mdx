Trepzy uses two distinct verification frameworks depending on whether the subject is a person or a company. Knowing which framework applies — and what completing it actually unlocks — prevents a common class of product and support errors.

## KYC: verifying an individual

**KYC** stands for *Know Your Customer*. It is the process of verifying that an individual is who they claim to be. A complete KYC process typically involves:

- Checking the person's identity document
- Validating the identification data provided
- Biometric confirmation that the person presenting the document is actually the person named in it

KYC applies to natural persons. It answers the question: *who is this individual?*

## KYB: verifying a business

**KYB** stands for *Know Your Business*. It is the process of verifying the legitimacy and structure of a company. A complete KYB process typically involves:

- Confirming that the company exists and is registered
- Understanding what activity the company conducts
- Identifying who represents the company
- Identifying who owns or controls the company

KYB applies to legal entities. It answers the question: *what is this company and who stands behind it?*

## What KYC and KYB do NOT automatically cover

This is where the most consequential mistakes happen. Keep these rules explicit:

<Warning>
- Completing KYC for a person does **not** automatically verify any company they work for or are associated with. Each company requires its own KYB process.
- Completing KYB for a company does **not** eliminate the need to separately verify the individuals linked to it. Each person who will act on the account may need their own KYC.
</Warning>

The two verifications are independent. One does not substitute for the other, and a result in one does not transfer to the other.

## AML: the layer above KYC and KYB

**AML** stands for *Anti-Money Laundering*. It is a broader layer of controls that sits on top of KYC and KYB. A verified identity — whether individual or business — does not automatically make every transaction that identity performs permitted.

Some situations require additional information before a movement can proceed, an outright refusal of the operation, or ongoing monitoring of activity over time. A clean KYC or KYB result is a prerequisite for many services, but it is not a guarantee that any specific movement will be allowed.

## Frequently asked questions

<Accordion title="Does passing KYC mean I can use all Trepzy features?">
No. Each feature has its own conditions that must be satisfied independently. KYC is one gate in the process, not a master key that unlocks everything. A feature may require additional information, a separate approval from a financial partner, or other conditions beyond a completed identity check.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can the same person's KYC be reused for a new service?">
Sometimes a valid proof can be reused for a subsequent service, avoiding the need to repeat the same document submission. However, the new service may still require additional information specific to it, and it will always require its own separate approval decision. Reusing a document does not mean the new service is automatically approved.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if my verification is incomplete?">
The operation or feature remains locked until all applicable conditions are met. Trepzy will indicate specifically what information is missing and who needs to act to move the process forward. A case sitting in an incomplete state is not automatically closed — there is always a clear next step.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does a company's KYB cover all its employees?">
No. KYB establishes facts about the company itself — its existence, activity, and controlling persons. Each individual employee or representative may still need to complete their own KYC verification, depending on the actions they will take within the account and the requirements of the relevant service.
</Accordion>
